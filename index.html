01 / About me

Beyond the numbers: my journey towards meaningful impact.

My journey began in Kenya, where I developed a deep appreciation for education, community-led development and access to opportunity.

With an academic foundation in Biostatistics, I began exploring how data could inform decisions, strengthen programmes and improve people's lives. My experiences across research, monitoring and evaluation, public institutions and international development revealed an important lesson: behind every statistic is a human story.

As a Mastercard Foundation Scholar at the University of Edinburgh, I pursued an MSc in Data, Inequality and Society, deepening my understanding of the relationships between data, power, technology and social justice.

Today, my work brings together research, evidence-informed decision-making, entrepreneurship and social innovation. As founder of ClimateClan360 and co-founder of Kakamega Women Telecenter, I am particularly interested in creating opportunities for women, young people and underserved communities through education, technology and sustainable development.

My professional journey across Kenya and the United Kingdom continues to shape my global perspective and commitment to inclusive innovation.

05 / Research & data analytics

Beyond the numbers: evidence for meaningful change.

I combine statistical analysis, qualitative inquiry and participatory evaluation to understand what works, for whom and why.

Featured research · MSc dissertation · 2025

The Stories Behind the Indicators

Centering Recipient Perspectives in Women’s Empowerment Programs in Kenya

University of Edinburgh · Edinburgh Futures Institute

Exploring the relationship between donor-defined indicators and women's lived experiences of empowerment.

7 completed interviews
2 research stages
4 interview coding families

Document analysis · Semi-structured interviews · Purposive and snowball sampling · Reflexive thematic analysis · NVivo 15

Explore the research themes

Meanings of empowerment

Confidence, rights awareness and individual circumstances shape how women understand empowerment.

Perceived programme impact

Participants described benefits alongside limitations of short-term interventions and longer-term needs.

Relevance and representation of indicators

Exploring whether measurement frameworks capture changes participants consider meaningful.

Inclusion, voice and power

Examining whose perspectives shape evaluation and how participants can contribute to more representative indicators.

Research limitations include purposive and snowball sampling, recall bias and researcher–participant power dynamics. Findings are contextual, not population estimates.

Social impact / Community leadership

Empowering communities. Expanding opportunities. Creating lasting change.

My commitment to social impact is rooted in the belief that where someone is born, their gender or their socioeconomic circumstances should not determine the opportunities available to them.

Through my work with Kakamega Women Telecenter and other community-focused organisations, I have contributed to initiatives that promote education, women's empowerment, digital inclusion, youth development and sustainable livelihoods.

A transformative milestone in my journey was my selection for the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program at the University of Edinburgh, where I pursued an MSc in Data, Inequality and Society. Becoming a Mastercard Foundation Scholar provided me with an opportunity to advance my education, strengthen my leadership capabilities and connect with a global community of changemakers committed to Africa's sustainable and inclusive development.

The scholarship reinforced my belief in the transformative power of education and equitable access to opportunity. It also strengthened my commitment to giving back, creating pathways for others and ensuring that the opportunities I have received translate into meaningful change for communities.

Through the Scholars Entrepreneurship Fund, I further developed this commitment into practical innovation through Climate Smart Dadas, an initiative connecting climate education, gender equality and community-led environmental action.

These experiences have shaped my approach to leadership, research and entrepreneurship. They have reinforced the importance of listening to communities, recognising local knowledge and developing solutions with people rather than simply for them.

My ambition is to connect grassroots initiatives with international networks, research, technology and sustainable funding to create meaningful and lasting opportunities for underserved communities.

I aspire to ensure that my journey is not defined solely by the opportunities I have received, but also by the opportunities I am able to create for others.
