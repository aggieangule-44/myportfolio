Featured research · MSc dissertation · 2025

The Stories Behind the Indicators

Centering Recipient Perspectives in Women’s Empowerment Programs in Kenya

University of Edinburgh · Edinburgh Futures Institute

Exploring the relationship between donor-defined indicators and women's lived experiences of empowerment.

7 completed interviews
2 research stages
5 interview analysis themes
30–60 minutes per interview

Document analysis · Semi-structured interviews · Purposive and snowball sampling · Reflexive thematic analysis · NVivo 15

Explore the research themes

Meanings of empowerment

Confidence, rights awareness and individual circumstances shape how women understand empowerment.

Perceived programme impact

Participants described benefits alongside limitations of short-term interventions and longer-term needs.

Relevance and representation of indicators

Exploring whether measurement frameworks capture changes participants consider meaningful.

Inclusion, voice and power

Examining whose perspectives shape evaluation and how participants can contribute to more representative indicators.

Challenges and disappointments

Short programme duration, barriers to participation and unmet needs highlighted the importance of sustained support.

Key findings from the final dissertation

Empowerment extended beyond donor-defined outputs to dignity, confidence, visibility, leadership and healing. Participation did not necessarily mean meaningful inclusion in programme design. The findings support more recipient-informed indicators and evaluation approaches that recognise relational and longer-term change.

In July 2025, seven completed WhatsApp interviews were analysed using Braun and Clarke’s six-phase approach and NVivo 15. One incomplete interview was excluded. Four initial analytical themes were supplemented by an inductively identified fifth theme.

Source: final MSc dissertation, 2025, printed pages 19–33. Findings are presented in aggregate to protect participants.

Research limitations include purposive and snowball sampling, recall bias and researcher–participant power dynamics. Findings are contextual, not population estimates.
